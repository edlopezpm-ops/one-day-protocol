# Understanding Your Mind

Part of [[Key Psychological Principles]].

- **Behaviour is goal-directed** — Nothing you do is random. Every pattern is aimed at something.
	- Procrastination, conflict, self-sabotage — each is efficiently achieving something: risk avoided, self-image preserved, a story kept intact. Ask what a behaviour is for, not why it is wrong.
- **Some of those goals work against you** — You can pursue a goal for years without ever having chosen it.
	- Goals get installed by family, school, peers and pain, then run silently. "Never look stupid" is a goal. So is "never need anyone." Either one will organise an entire life around itself.
- **Change the goal, not just the schedule** — New perspective produces new goals, which produce new behaviour.
	- Optimising your schedule around an unexamined goal only makes you more efficient at the wrong thing. Perspective sets the goal, the goal sets attention, attention sets behaviour. Intervene at the top.
- **Identity defends itself** — Your sense of self reacts to contradiction the way your body reacts to infection.
	- Evidence that contradicts who you think you are registers as a threat, not as information. That is why good advice bounces off: the immune response fires before the argument is even heard.
