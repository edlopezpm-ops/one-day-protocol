# Workbook

Answer underneath each question. Nothing here leaves your machine.

## Part 1 — Morning: Psychological Excavation

*15–30 minutes of honest writing, before the day gets loud.*

### Anti-Vision Questions

**What dissatisfaction have you quietly learned to tolerate?**

*Tolerance is the trap. Anything unbearable gets fixed — it is the merely annoying that lasts a decade. List what you have stopped noticing.*

> 

**What do you complain about repeatedly and never change?**

*A complaint you repeat is a decision you have already made. Naming it removes the option of pretending it is still an open question.*

> 

**What true thing about your life would be unbearable to say out loud?**

*Write the sentence you would never say at dinner. If nothing comes, you are editing — the resistance itself is the location.*

> 

**Five years out, nothing changes. Describe an ordinary Tuesday.**

*Not a summary — an hour-by-hour Tuesday. Where you wake up, what is in front of you, who is there, what you feel at 3pm. Vagueness is how you protect yourself here.*

> 

**Ten years out, same trajectory. Which doors have closed for good?**

*Some doors close by age, some by accumulated commitments, some by skills you did not build in time. Name which ones, specifically.*

> 

**At the end of your life, what did you never once attempt?**

*Strip out the social scoring. What did you actually want that you never made a single real attempt at?*

> 

**Which identity would you have to stop defending in order to change?**

*Every change costs an identity — the realist, the one who had it hard, the one everyone relies on. Naming the price is what makes it payable.*

> 

**What is the embarrassing, real reason you have not changed yet?**

*Not the respectable reason. The one that sounds petty out loud: fear of looking stupid, of losing an excuse, of succeeding and still feeling like this.*

> 

### Vision Questions

**Three years out, best realistic case. Describe an ordinary Tuesday in detail.**

*Best realistic case, not fantasy. Detail is what makes it usable: the hour you wake, the work in front of you, who you eat with, what you are no longer worried about.*

> 

**What would you have to believe about yourself for that life to feel normal?**

*Beliefs are load-bearing. That life stays out of reach for as long as you hold beliefs that make it absurd for you, specifically, to have it.*

> 

**If you were already that person, what would you do this week?**

*This converts the vision into something testable within seven days. Small enough to actually do, and unmistakably that person's move.*

> 

## Part 2 — Throughout the Day: Pattern Interruption

*Six alarms that catch you mid-behaviour, plus three questions to carry around.*

### Phone reminders

**11:00 — What am I avoiding right now?**

*Whatever came to mind first is the answer. Avoidance attaches itself to the thing that matters most, not the thing that is hardest.*

> 

**13:30 — Judging only by my behaviour today, what would a stranger say I want?**

*Your stated goals are not evidence. Your calendar and your last four hours are.*

> 

**15:15 — Is this moving me toward the life I want or the one I dread?**

*Binary question, no third option. Neutral is drift, and drift defaults to the life you dread.*

> 

**17:00 — What important thing am I pretending is not important?**

*Usually a health issue, a relationship, or a conversation. Downgrading it in your head is cheaper than acting on it.*

> 

**19:30 — Today, what did I do to protect my image rather than to get what I want?**

*Image protection is invisible from the inside. Look for the moment you softened something, stayed quiet, or performed.*

> 

**21:00 — When did I feel most alive today? Most dead?**

*Track this across several days. Aliveness is data about direction, not a mood to chase.*

> 

### Carry-around questions

**What changes if I stop needing people to see me as ______?**

*Fill the blank with the label you work hardest to maintain. Most of what that identity costs you stays invisible until you price it.*

> 

**Where am I trading aliveness for safety?**

*Safety is not the enemy; unexamined safety is. Find the specific trade you keep making without deciding to.*

> 

**What is the smallest version of my future self I could be tomorrow?**

*Not a transformation — the smallest recognisable version. One hour, one decision, one different answer.*

> 

## Part 3 — Evening: Synthesis

*Turn the day's raw material into two sentences and three goals.*

### Integration questions

**Why, specifically, have you been stuck?**

*Answer with a mechanism, not a mood. "I lacked discipline" is not an answer. "I never risked being bad at it in front of people" is.*

> 

**Name the internal enemy: which pattern or belief is actually running this?**

*It is a pattern or a belief — never a person or a circumstance. If your answer is external, you have nothing to act on.*

> 

**One sentence: what you refuse to let your life become.**

*One sentence. If it takes a paragraph you have not found it yet. This is the line you will re-read on the bad days.*

> 

**One sentence: what you are building toward.**

*One sentence, stated positively, in your own words. Not a goal — a direction.*

> 

### Goal setting — lenses, not finish lines

**1 year: what would have to be true for you to know the pattern is broken?**

*Pick evidence, not a feeling. Something an outside observer could confirm twelve months from now.*

> 

**1 month: what must be true this month to keep the 1-year goal possible?**

*This is the constraint that keeps the year honest — what has to be true in 30 days for the 1-year answer to still be on the table.*

> 

**Daily: which 2–3 actions would your future self simply do, without debate?**

*Two or three, not ten. They have to be doable on a bad day, or they are not levers.*

> 

## Six components to document

This is the deliverable. Keep it where you will actually see it.

### Anti-vision

*The life you refuse to end up in.* — What is at stake if you lose.

Write it as a scene, not a fear. You will re-read this on the days you do not feel like doing the work — that is its only job.

> 

### Vision

*The life you are building toward.* — The win condition.

A direction, not a destination. Concrete enough that you would recognise it if you arrived.

> 

### 1-year goal

*What is true twelve months from now.* — The main mission.

One sentence, verifiable. If you could argue about whether you hit it, it is still too vague.

> 

### 1-month project

*The skill to acquire or the thing to build.* — The boss fight, where the XP comes from.

Something that ships or is learned inside 30 days. It should scare you slightly and produce evidence either way.

> 

### Daily levers

*The 2–3 tasks that actually move it.* — Daily quests.

Two or three tasks, done before anything else. These are the only things that must happen for a day to count.

> 

### Constraints

*What you will not sacrifice to get it.* — The rules that force creativity.

Health, relationships, ethics, sleep — whatever you will not trade. Constraints are not obstacles; they force the creative path.
