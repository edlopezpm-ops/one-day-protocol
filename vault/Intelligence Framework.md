# Intelligence Framework

Part of [[Key Psychological Principles]].

- **Intelligence = getting what you actually want** — A working definition, not an IQ score.
	- Can you look at your situation, see it accurately, and move it toward what you want? People with unremarkable credentials do this constantly. People with impressive ones often cannot.
- **Three ingredients: agency, opportunity, intelligence** — Remove any one and results stall.
	- Agency without opportunity is frustration. Opportunity without agency is waste. Both, without the ability to steer, becomes a well-rehearsed story about bad luck.
- **The cybernetic loop** — Set a goal, act, sense where you are, compare to the goal, adjust.
	- The comparison step is the one everyone skips. Acting without measuring the gap between where you are and where you meant to be is motion, not progress.
- **High intelligence = iteration speed** — Persisting and learning from mistakes beats being right early.
	- The advantage compounds: more attempts means more feedback, and more feedback means better aim. Being right on attempt one is luck. Being right by attempt twelve is skill.
